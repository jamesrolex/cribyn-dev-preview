Case studies

What we were asked, and what it took.

Each of these is described at the level of the question, not the client. Sectors, jurisdictions and outcomes are real; names, dates and anything that could identify a party are not given. Where a matter is still live, it does not appear here at all.

Technology distribution · CEE
A distributor who looked clean on paper

Instructed by a technology vendor before appointing a regional distributor. Registry work in a third jurisdiction showed the operating company held through an intermediate structure whose principal was named in live proceedings not visible in the local record. We advised against appointment.

Level three · three weeks
Energy · Gulf
Site photographs offered as proof of capacity

Instructed by an investor ahead of a facility financing. The counterparty supplied geotagged imagery of the plant. Provenance analysis and independent satellite radar were run on each exhibit before anyone relied on it; the finding attached to the images and travelled with the report.

Level four · ten days
Mining · Sub-Saharan Africa
How a licence was really granted

Instructed by private equity on a target holding a single concession. The question was not whether the licence existed but on whose authority it was issued, and what the local partner had been promised. Answered through in-country sources, graded and reported separately from the documentary record.

Level three · four weeks
Disputes · former Soviet space
Assets that moved after judgment

Instructed by counsel enforcing an award. Holdings had been restructured through two jurisdictions after the ruling. Filings read at source in the original language, and the chain reconstructed to a standard the enforcement court could rely on.

Level four · six weeks
Financial services · UK
A board appointment, checked before announcement

Instructed by a nominations committee. Commercial record, standing with regulators and personal probity, built from people who had worked alongside the candidate. Nothing adverse was found, and the report said so with the same care it would have taken to say otherwise.

Level three · two weeks
Manufacturing · Southeast Asia
Where the supply chain actually ended

Instructed by a manufacturer under new export-control obligations. The question was whether a distributor's onward customers were who the paperwork said. Sources at two points in the chain gave a different answer from the documents; both were reported, with grades.

Level three · three weeks
