Intelligence

Anyone can gather information. The work is knowing what it is worth.

We are instructed when a decision cannot be made on the papers alone: a counterparty who looks clean, a structure that does not quite explain itself, a claim that has to hold up if it is ever challenged. Every mandate runs the same way, and it runs in this order.

First, the question has to be worth asking.
01 · The mandate

A vague brief produces a vague report. We spend the first conversation narrowing the question to the one whose answer would change your decision — and we will tell you when that answer cannot be obtained, or cannot be obtained lawfully, before you commit a budget to finding out.

Scope, timetable and cost are agreed in writing. The partner who agrees them is the partner who runs the work.

Then it has to be current.
02 · The record

Every mandate starts from nothing. Registry filings are pulled in the original language and read there, not through a translated summary; court records are obtained from the court, not from an aggregator that indexed them two years ago. Where a database is the only route, we say which database and when it was last refreshed.

Recycled research is worse than none: it carries the confidence of a finished report and the age of a stale one.

Most of what matters is not written down. That is HUMINT.
03 · Human sources
Sources →

Whether a licence was really granted on merit, whether a supplier relationship is what the contract says it is, whether a director is trusted by the people who have dealt with him — none of that is in a filing. It sits with regulators, former colleagues, competitors, counsel and journalists who have watched the same market for twenty years.

More than five hundred such people can be tasked through us, each held by a named handler and bound under English law. Every enquiry is made lawfully, without pretext against regulated data, and with your identity protected throughout.

But sources can be mistaken, and documents can now be manufactured.
04 · Verification
How we verify →

This is the part nobody else is doing. A generative model can now produce a convincing photograph of a real place in the time it takes to describe it, and a good source can pass on material they were given in good faith. So every file entering a case room is checked for what it says about its own origin, and where it claims a location, independent satellite radar is asked whether anything is there.

Absence of metadata is reported as unknown origin, never as evidence of tampering. Verification protects the source as much as it protects you.

Only then is it worth writing down.
05 · The report

Findings are separated from inference and both are separated from speculation. Every line carries a source grade and an information credibility grade, recorded independently, so you can see how much weight it is carrying before you rely on it. What we could not establish is stated as plainly as what we could.

The report is written to be put in front of an investment committee, a regulator or a court — and to survive the question “how do you know that?”
