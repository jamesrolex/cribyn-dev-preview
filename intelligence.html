Intelligence

Anyone can gather information. The work is knowing what it means.

We are instructed when a decision cannot be made on the papers alone: a counterparty who looks clean, a structure that does not quite explain itself, a claim that has to hold up if it is ever challenged. Every mandate runs the same way, and it runs in this order.

How a mandate runs
01
First, the question has to be worth asking.
Scope, cost and timetable agreed in writing. We say when an answer cannot be obtained, or cannot be obtained lawfully, before you commit a budget.
02
Then it has to be current.
Every mandate starts from nothing. Filings and court records read at source, in the original language. Recycled research is worse than none.
03
Most of what matters is not written down. That is HUMINT.
Regulators, former colleagues, competitors, counsel — tasked lawfully by a named handler, recorded in their own words, graded A–F and 1–6.
04
But sources can be mistaken, and documents can now be made.
Provenance analysis on every exhibit; location claims put to satellite radar. Absence of metadata is reported as unknown origin, never as tampering.
05
Only then is it worth writing down.
Findings separated from inference, every line graded, and what we could not establish stated as plainly as what we could.
Four levels of enquiry

Four levels of enquiry. Be clear which one you are commissioning.

Most of what is sold as due diligence is a database search with a cover sheet. That is sometimes all that is needed, and we will say so. But the term covers four quite different levels of analysis, as demonstrated below, each including the one before it, and the difference between them is where the risk sits.

Level one
Screening
A compliance answer: is this name on a list. Fast, cheap, and only as good as the lists.
Sanctions and PEP databases
Adverse media aggregators
Corporate registry extracts
Watchlist and enforcement lists
Commission this when
A regulator requires it, or the counterparty is low-value and low-exposure.
Level two
OSINT
Open-source intelligence: everything public, found by a trained analyst and read in the original language.
All of level one, plus
Filings and court records at source
Ownership and structure mapping
Litigation and insolvency history
Open media and social, original language
Deep-web and archive retrieval
Commission this when
You need to know who you are dealing with and the record is reasonably reliable where they operate.
Level three
HUMINT
Human-source intelligence: what is not written down, from people in the jurisdiction who know the subject first-hand.
All of level two, plus
Discreet enquiry through trusted sources
Subject-matter and sector advisors
Reputation with regulators and peers
Undeclared relationships and liabilities
Source graded A–F, information 1–6
Commission this when
The public record is thin, the stake is material, or a person's judgement is the thing you are actually backing.
Level four
Verified intelligence
Level three, with every document and image tested before it reaches the report.
All of level three, plus
Provenance analysis on every exhibit
Generative-media and edit detection
Location claims put to satellite radar

Findings prepared to be relied upon

Verification

Anyone can assert a photograph is real.
We can show our working.

Generative tools can now produce a convincing image of a real place. So every file entering a Cribyn case room is put through the same four steps, and the result is recorded against the evidence — not asserted in a covering note.

Step one
Sealed on arrival

The file is hashed and encrypted at rest the moment it reaches the case room, with an immutable record of who put it there.

Step two
What it says of itself

Content Credentials, capture metadata, editing history, and any declaration that a generative model made or altered it.

Step three
Asked of others

Where an image claims a place, independent sources are queried for the same coordinates — including satellite radar, which is far harder to fabricate than a picture.

Step four
Recorded, not claimed

The finding attaches to the exhibit and travels with the report, so the position can be examined rather than taken on trust.

A worked exhibit
Exhibit 04 · Is this facility real and operating? · Houston Ship Channel
Corroborated
What we were given
A photograph from the counterparty. Geotagged, undated, offered as proof the site is operating.
What the satellite saw
Sentinel-1 radar · same coordinates · not a photograph
Green outlines mark what the analyst identified in the radar and matched to the photograph · bright = built structure · dark = water
The radar image is not a photograph. The satellite sends a signal down and records what bounces back: hard, built things return brightly, water returns almost nothing. That cannot be faked with an image tool, which is why it is the second opinion we reach for first. Illustrative example, assembled from open data: aerial photograph public domain (Carol M. Highsmith, Library of Congress); radar contains modified Copernicus Sentinel data 2026.
This is provenance analysis, not pixel forensics. Absence of metadata is reported as unknown origin — messaging platforms and content systems strip it routinely — and is never presented as evidence of tampering.
